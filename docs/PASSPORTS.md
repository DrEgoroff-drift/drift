# Design passports — the small things are the work (M150)

Not final polish: an entry condition. Half of the thirteenth pass rests on a needle looking like a
needle from a factory rather than like a UI element. This file is the checklist every new object,
instrument, label and sound is read against before it is drawn. It is a rules document: the code
that already follows it is in `25a`–`25d` (instruments), `27f` (the HQ room), `21aa`/`21ab` (base
rooms), `20c` (the meadow) and `11*` (regions); anything new is held to the same four questions.

## The provenance rule (rooms → props)

Every object answers four questions or it does not get made:

1. **what it is for**,
2. **what it is made of**,
3. **who made it and when**,
4. **what is written on it**.

No object exists because it looks nice.

## Instruments

- Dial gauges, never digital. Round and rectangular scales, glass with a highlight and a scratch, a
  brass bezel, the cockpit reflected in it.
- Every instrument is the product of a specific works: its own scale, its own digit shapes, its own
  needle manner (`05b-instr-kit`). A new instrument's needle stands dead; an old one's floats.
- **Instruments never speak.** Not one beep, not one colour change, not one alert. The misclosure
  window is the only detector, and it is a number.

## Labels

Stencilled, Russian, clerical, unglamorous: `ХРОНОМЕТР`, `НЕВЯЗКА`, an inventory number. No icons,
no pictograms — words and numbers only.

## Palette and material

Painted metal in three shades (light grey, pale green, ochre), enamel chipped through to primer,
brass, ebonite, glass, canvas, and wood wherever a person made something for himself. Warm
filament light inside against cold stars outside — the only permanent colour conflict in frame.
No neon, no blue holograms.

## Four wear layers on every object

factory → use (rubbed where hands go, soot by the exhaust) → repair (someone else's part in the
wrong colour) → **personal** (a sticker, a scratch, a nickname on the hull, somebody's drawing).
The fourth layer is what separates a world people lived in from a world that was generated.

## One type family

Stencil for machinery, typewriter for documents, handwriting for notes. All Russian, all legible.
On alien objects, not an "alien font" but **no writing at all** — silence instead of ornament
(the meadow, the grove, the valley, the three hand-placed places carry no label).

## Sound

Quieter than is usual. A constant bed of hum, the recorder's pen, relay clicks, ether noise between
stations. Instruments make no sound whatsoever. Music is rare and almost always diegetic.

## People

Work clothes in layers, pocketed and mended. A real range of ages. No armour, no faction uniforms;
a county is told apart by knitted versus canvas, by a cap versus a headscarf, by what has been used
to patch the elbow.

## Rules the thirteenth pass adds (kept here so they are read)

- The world does not congratulate: no "mystery solved", no chime, no counter.
- One explanation per region, and never in text.
- The reward is access, fuel, a coordinate, a changed place. Not credits.
- Nothing scales to the player. An event is never rescheduled to suit an arrival.
